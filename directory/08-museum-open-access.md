# Museum open-access vaults

Museums around the world have quietly released millions of works as CC0 or public
domain. These are the highest-curation art sources on this page: expert lighting,
professional scans, and every object page states the rights.

| Source | What's inside | License notes | Safe to sell | Link |
|--------|---------------|---------------|--------------|------|
| Smithsonian Open Access | 5+ million objects (art, history, science, specimens) with 4.5M+ tagged CC0. Includes the natural-history plates that pair with BHL. | CC0 on tagged items - verified per object page. | SAFE | https://www.si.edu/openaccess |
| The Met Open Access | The Met's public-domain holdings (all eras) as CC0, with high-res downloads and an easy "public domain" filter. | CC0 on "Open Access" items. | SAFE | https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection |
| Met open-access policy page | The Met's formal policy statement, useful if you ever document your rights decision. | Policy reference. | N/A | https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/open-access |
| Art Institute of Chicago | Hundreds of thousands of works with IIIF high-res downloads; enormous public-domain share. | Rights panel per work; PD/CC0 items clearly marked. | CHECK | https://www.artic.edu/collection |
| Rijksmuseum Rijksstudio | Dutch Golden Age masters, botanical still lifes, decorative arts; famously CC0-friendly. | Public-domain works offered CC0; per-item panel. | SAFE | https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/rijksstudio |
| Cleveland Museum of Art Open Access | Full collection released CC0 (one of the first museums to go all-in) with high-res downloads. | CC0. | SAFE | https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access |

Also note: the Library of Congress "Free to Use & Reuse" program (in its own section of
this directory) works like a museum open-access program - pre-cleared, curated sets
with zero license guesswork.

## Practical tips

- Sort/filter by "Public domain" or "CC0" first; nearly every museum site lets you.
- Download the largest offered resolution (some offer the original master file), then
  scale down for covers; you will want the detail for 300-dpi print work.
- Museum object pages give you a citation string already - paste it on your copyright
  page when attribution applies.
