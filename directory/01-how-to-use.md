# How to use this directory

This is a working directory of free, public-domain (PD) and CC0 art sources, curated for
KDP book covers, low-content printables, and other products you sell. It is a link
directory - no scraping, no downloading at scale, no mass re-hosting. You browse the
source, pick an image, confirm its license tag, and use it.

## The safety flags

| Flag | Meaning | Can it go on a KDP cover or printable you sell? |
|------|---------|------------------------------------------------|
| SAFE | The source states the material is public domain or CC0 (often also copyright-free designations like NASA's "mostly unrestricted"). Use freely, no attribution needed. | Yes, as-is. |
| SAFE-ATTR | Material is free for commercial use but attribution is required (CC BY) or strongly customary. | Yes, as long as you include the credit (usually on the copyright page or under the image). |
| SAFE-COND | Commercial use is allowed under the site's own license, with stated conditions (e.g., don't redistribute the files as-is as stock). Read the license page once and follow it. | Yes, if you stay inside the stated conditions. |
| CHECK | Mixed-license source. Individual items vary (some CC0, some CC BY, some restricted). You verify each item's license tag before use. | Yes, item by item, once you verify the tag. |
| NO | Do not use commercially. (No source in this directory carries this flag; it exists so the legend is complete.) | No. |

## Practical workflow for a KDP cover

1. Browse a source below and download an image you like.
2. Check the license tag next to the image (Wikimedia, the Met, and LOC all show rights
   statements per item).
3. If the flag is SAFE, you are done. If SAFE-ATTR, add a credit line to your copyright
   page (this is normal and expected - it does not clash with KDP).
4. If CHECK, confirm the tag says public domain, "no known copyright restrictions",
   CC0, or an open license you understand - then proceed.
5. Keep a tiny credits note per book (source URL + license). Two minutes now saves a
   headache later.

## Honest caveats (read once, then relax)

- **Public domain = sure.** Works whose copyright has expired (usually pre-1930 in the US)
  are PD. Photographic faithful reproductions of 2D PD art are PD in the US (see
  Bridgeman v. Corel); some countries differ, but selling on Amazon KDP US is US law.
- **"No known restrictions" = good but verify.** Library of Congress uses this tag. It
  means the library found no evidence of copyright restrictions, andLOC marks these
  items in the Free to Use sets specifically so you do not have to dig deeper.
- **AI re-colorization or restoration is on you.** If a scan comes from a source that
  licenses only the scan, keep your edits to the scan simple or choose a cleaner source.
- **Never re-sell a source's files as-is in bulk.** That is redistribution, the one thing
  most "free" licenses forbid (and it is against KDP spirit anyway). Use them inside
  your own design.

## What is NOT in here, on purpose

- Sites whose terms allow "personal use only" (they fail the safe-to-sell test).
- Sites that require scraping or login to check licenses.
- Anything with unclear or hostile commercial terms.
